# How do I position my brand?
> **In short:** Positioning defines the market you compete in, who you're for, what makes you different and why that matters to the buyer. Messaging architecture turns it into the exact words used on your website, in decks, in ads and on sales calls.

## You might be seeing
- Your homepage could be swapped with a competitor's and nobody would notice
- Every team describes the product differently
- Prospects understand *what* you do but not *why it matters to them*
- A new product launch with no clear story

## What's usually happening
Positioning gets written once, often by the founder, and never tested against the buyer. As the product grows, the story fragments. Your competitors aren't necessarily better. They may just be positioned better.

## How we do it
1. Gather inputs from your ICP, competitive set and customer language
2. Run a positioning workshop: category, alternatives, unique value, proof
3. Draft 2–3 positioning territories and test them with real buyers
4. Build the messaging architecture: pillars, proof points, value by persona
5. Roll it out to the website, deck and sales talk track

## Frequently asked questions
### What is B2B positioning?
B2B positioning defines how a product is understood relative to the alternatives a buyer is considering: the market category, the target customer, the unique value and the proof behind it. It is the foundation for messaging, sales conversations and marketing.

### What is a messaging framework?
A messaging framework (or messaging house) organises the core value proposition, three to four supporting pillars, proof points for each, and value statements tailored to each buyer persona, so every team communicates consistently.

### Is brand positioning the same as a rebrand?
No. Positioning is strategy. Visual identity stays unless you want to change it.

### How do you test positioning?
Through buyer interviews, message testing on LinkedIn ads and landing-page A/B tests.
