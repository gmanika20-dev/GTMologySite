# Stop using AI as a typewriter

**In short:** Most marketing teams use AI to write faster. The real leverage is redesigning multi-step marketing work, such as research, audits, reporting and enablement, as AI workflows with clear stages, guardrails and a human approval step. Start with one repetitive, high-value job, measure the hours it saves, then scale.

Walk into almost any B2B marketing team and ask how they use AI. The answer is usually some version of: *"We use ChatGPT to draft LinkedIn posts and blog outlines."*

That's useful. It's also the smallest possible version of what AI can do for a marketing operation. It's AI as a typewriter, a faster way to produce the same artefacts through the same process.

## The shift: from tasks to jobs

A **task** is "write a paragraph." A **job** is "produce a competitive-intelligence report our sales team can act on."

Jobs have stages. A competitive report, for example, involves identifying the real competitors, benchmarking positioning, comparing features, reading reviews, analysing SEO, synthesising a SWOT, writing battle cards, and recommending actions. A person needs days or weeks for that. A well-designed AI workflow can run every stage in sequence, with each stage feeding the next.

I learned this building my own competitive-intelligence product: a **multi-stage AI pipeline** that turns a research cycle that used to take weeks into a structured report in minutes. The model wasn't the hard part. The hard part was the **process design**, which only came from having done the research by hand many times: what each stage needs, what it must produce, and where a human has to check the output.

## Five marketing jobs that are ready for agents

| Job today | Agent version | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Competitive research | Research agent with a stage-by-stage pipeline | Days → minutes. Intel refreshed monthly, not yearly |
| Content audits | Audit agent that scores every page against your criteria | A one-off project becomes a continuous check |
| Newsletter production | Curation + drafting workflow in your brand voice | Hours → a 15-minute review |
| Performance reporting | Automated data pull + AI-written commentary | Spreadsheet stitching disappears |
| Sales enablement | Account brief generated before every call | Reps walk in prepared |

## The design rules that make it work

1. **Pick a job, not a tool.** Start from a recurring, painful, well-understood piece of work.
2. **Map the stages first.** Write down how a great human does the job, step by step. That becomes the agent's architecture.
3. **Define inputs and outputs per stage.** Vague stages produce vague output.
4. **Keep a human in the loop where judgement matters.** Approval steps are a feature, not a weakness, especially in regulated industries like financial services.
5. **Measure hours saved and cost per run.** If you can't measure it, you can't defend it to leadership.
6. **Teach it your voice.** An AI that doesn't know your positioning will produce fluent, generic output.

## Why this matters more for small teams

A five-person marketing team can't hire an analyst, a research specialist and a reporting manager. But it can run agents that do much of that work, so the humans spend their time on strategy, relationships and judgement.

That's the real promise: **AI as a team member that completes whole jobs**, not a faster keyboard.


## Key takeaways
- Using AI only to draft content is the smallest version of its value.
- Redesign whole multi-step jobs (research, audits, reporting, enablement) as AI workflows.
- Map the stages, keep a human approval step, and measure hours saved and cost per run.

## Frequently asked questions
### How can B2B marketing teams use AI beyond content writing?
By building AI workflows that complete multi-step jobs such as competitive research, content audits, newsletter production, performance reporting and account briefs, with a human approving the output.

### What is the difference between an AI agent and a chatbot?
A chatbot answers individual prompts. An AI agent works through a defined sequence of stages, using tools and data, to complete a whole job and produce a finished output.

### Where should a marketing team start with AI agents?
Start with one recurring, time-consuming, well-understood job. Map how a skilled person does it step by step, build the agent around those stages, and measure the hours it saves.
