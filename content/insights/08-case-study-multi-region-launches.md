# One Story, Three Regions: Bringing a Portfolio of Lending Software Launches to Market

**In short:** A global banking-technology SaaS provider needed to launch a series of lending products and features across the US, EMEA and APAC. By agreeing one positioning per launch and a repeatable launch asset blueprint, the team aligned product owners, subject-matter experts and regional marketers, and gave sales consistent material for every release.

> This case study describes work Manika Gupta led in-house in a previous role. The company is not named.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Company** | A global banking-technology provider with a lending software portfolio |
| **Scope** | A portfolio of product and feature launches across the US, EMEA and APAC |
| **Challenge** | Many stakeholders, different regional needs and no shared launch structure |
| **Approach** | Launch positioning for each release and a standard asset blueprint |
| **Result** | Consistent launch stories, faster asset production and sales-ready materials for each launch |

## The challenge

Each launch involved product owners with deep technical knowledge, subject-matter experts with strong views on how the product should be described, regional teams with different market priorities, and sales teams who needed something they could use with bank customers.

Without a shared structure, every launch started from a blank page. Messages varied by region and by author, assets were produced in a different order each time, and sales sometimes learned about new capabilities late.

## The approach

### 1. Positioning before assets
For each launch, the team first agreed who the release was for, the business problem it solved for banks, and how it compared with alternatives. Only then did asset work begin.

### 2. A repeatable asset blueprint
A standard set of launch assets was defined, so every release followed the same path from positioning to the market.

### 3. Align stakeholders early
Product owners and subject experts reviewed the positioning, not every individual asset. This moved debate to the start of the process, where it was cheapest, and sped up approvals later.

### 4. Equip sales for each release
Every launch included a sales play and battle card, so customer-facing teams could explain the release and how it compared with competitors from day one.

### 5. Use AI to speed up research and production
AI tools supported competitor gap analysis, customer needs research and demo video scripting, with people reviewing and approving the results.

## The result

- **Consistent messages** across regions and channels for every launch
- **A repeatable process** that cut the time spent restarting each launch from scratch
- **Sales-ready material** available at launch rather than after it
- **Fewer review cycles**, because the main debates happened once, at the positioning stage

## Key takeaways
- Agree positioning before producing any launch asset.
- A standard asset blueprint turns each launch into a repeatable process.
- Involve experts in the story once, rather than in every asset.
- Ship sales enablement with the launch, not after it.
